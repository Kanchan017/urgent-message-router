# Urgent Message Labelling Guide

## Critical

Use Critical when a message suggests possible immediate danger to life, health or safety.

Examples:

- fire or smoke;
- inability to breathe;
- unconscious person;
- violence or immediate threat;
- serious electrical danger;
- essential medical equipment failure.

## Urgent

Use Urgent when prompt human attention is needed but immediate danger is not clear.

Examples:

- essential service failure;
- delayed medication;
- no water or electricity;
- urgent welfare problem;
- repeated unresolved serious complaint.

## Routine

Use Routine for normal administrative or information requests.

Examples:

- address changes;
- forms;
- opening hours;
- general information;
- standard appointments.

## Uncertain

Use Uncertain when there is not enough information to determine the urgency.

Examples:

- “Please help me.”
- “Something happened.”
- “Contact me about a problem.”

## Main labelling rule

Assign the label based on the meaning of the message. Do not lower the urgency because of spelling, grammar or plain English.
